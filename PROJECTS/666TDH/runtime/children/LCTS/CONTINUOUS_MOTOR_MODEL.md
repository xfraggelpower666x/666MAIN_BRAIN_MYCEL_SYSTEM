# Continuous Motor Model

## Core relation

`MOTOR supports LIFE`

`LIFE transforms WITHOUT DESTROYING MOTOR`

The motor is temporal certainty, not creative emptiness.

## Motor contents
Typical motor identity records:
- BPM;
- kick timing and envelope;
- bass pulse/subdivision;
- bass envelope and low-end space;
- fundamental groove;
- bar and phrase grammar;
- transient relationship;
- low-end stereo discipline.

## Stability target
Across extensions, preserve perceived shared time:
- stable BPM;
- stable kick spacing;
- stable bass pulse;
- predictable bar boundaries;
- phrase continuity;
- no accidental swing/reset unless the track's established motor already contains it.

## What may evolve above the motor
- mid percussion;
- Goa structures;
- acid axes;
- forest microtextures;
- spectral friction;
- voices;
- granular responses;
- atmosphere;
- psychoacoustic space;
- phase/proximity events;
- emotional pressure;
- controlled chaos.

## Important distinction

Perceived acceleration created by upper subdivisions or density can be valid.

Actual low-end temporal instability that makes beatmatching unreliable is drift.

`PSYCHOACOUSTIC_TIME_DISTORTION != REAL_TEMPO_DISTORTION`

## Trustworthy does not mean dead

The motor may live micro-dynamically through:
- subtle timbral evolution;
- minute pressure movement;
- controlled filtering;
- fine texture variation;
- overtone change.

Those changes are subordinate to temporal identity.

`STABLE_DOES_NOT_MEAN_DEAD`

`STABLE_MEANS_TRUSTWORTHY`

Reject as motor drift when not source-earned:
- beat displacement;
- bass-rhythm reinvention;
- perceived tempo wave created by low-end instability;
- unstable phrase length;
- arbitrary break-like motor collapse.
